Town Forge is an open-world sandbox RPG with a town to build at its heart. Train any skill just by using it, raise your homestead into a walled settlement that keeps working while you’re away, and hold it against a wilderness of monsters, rival adventurers and orc war bands.

Starting town
Emberhold

You start in Emberhold, dressed and carrying a little gold. A short First steps guide walks you through the basics one goal at a time: walk around, visit a shop, head out the town gate, gather from a tree or rock, fight a monster and loot it. You can skip it at any time.

While you are Young (your first 20 minutes of play, plus the whole First steps guide), Murder Inc., ambushes and orc war parties leave you alone, and monsters notice you from closer in.

A good first hour

  • Gather wood and ore in the Whisperwood just outside Emberhold to train Lumberjacking and Mining.
  • Fight grey and green monsters (see threat colors) until your weapon skill passes 30.
  • Buy reagents at the Provisioner and put Spark Dart and Mending Word on your spell bar.
  • Save for a house plot, then start a Settlement. It works for you while you're offline.

The MENU

Every screen has one MENU button at the top left. It shows your resources and groups every page into a few buttons:

  • Me & Skills: your paperdoll, backpack and stats, and your skill loadout.
  • Craft, Magic, Pets: the workshops, your spellbook and your pets.
  • Bank & House: your bank box, your home and settlement. If you are short of gold at a shop, the rest comes out of your bank for a 2% fee.
  • Help & Save: the walkthrough, Cloud save and Reset (tap Reset twice to confirm).
  • Guild, Quests (the Quest Board: wanted posters, town tasks, weekly goals and your treasure maps) and Options (music on/off, calm or lively music, music and sound volume, sound effects, always-daytime, classic body, alerts and tap-to-walk).

Inside a group, the tabs at the top switch between its pages. The gold > Play button next to MENU always takes you back to where you were in the world.

Inside a crafting shop or your home workshop, every crafting station has a floating sign ("Smith here"). Walk up to it and a gold ring pulses on the floor; tap the button to start crafting.

Messages pop up just above your spell bar: orange for warnings, green for good news. Every hit and miss is kept in the journal.

Visiting a town marks it, so you can Recall there later. You walk into a town through its gatehouse, and into dungeons through their doorways.

Town buildings are half-timbered or brick, with glazed windows that glow with lamplight after dark, shutters, hung doors and stone footings. From the wilds each town is a walled town of dressed stone: crenellated walls, round towers with tiled roofs and pennants in the town's colours, and a gatehouse with torches, banners and a raised portcullis.

The world doesn't stop at the edge of the map. Past the border the land carries on: the Stonepeaks climb into grey mountains to the west, the Frostwastes into snowy peaks to the north, wooded hills roll away to the south, and the open sea stretches east. You can't walk there, but it's there on the horizon.

Regions

Rivers, the lake and rock ridges block your way; cross at bridges and fords. The King's Road links the towns, and its patrols make monsters slower to notice you while you're on it.

Landmarks

  • Grimtusk Hold: the orc fortress in the south. Strike one orc and the whole hold answers. The Orc Warlord drops the Warlord's Cleaver. The orcs are fully animated: they walk, charge, swing and fall.
  • The Fields of Sorrow: a haunted battlefield where Sorrow Wraiths roam.
  • The Cinder Caldera: far south-east, lair of the Tri-Wyrm.
  • Virtue shrines: seven across the land. A ghost can pray at one to come back to life.
  • Murder Inc.'s war camp: a palisade camp. It moves if it would sit too close to your home.

Your hero

You play as a fully animated, sculpted hero. He walks, runs and creeps when hidden. He takes a combat stance in a fight. He slashes, strings together combos, swings two-handed weapons and looses arrows, and casts spells two ways. He parries when you block, staggers when hit, and falls when killed. He swings an axe at trees and a pick at rock, and kneels while he meditates. When he rides, he sits in the saddle.

  • Strength is your hit points (STR 60 means 60 HP) and adds melee damage.
  • Dexterity makes you swing faster, dodge more blows and parry better. At 100 DEX you swing about every 0.4 seconds; bonus DEX past 100 gets you down to 0.35.

Stats rise on their own as you use related skills: fighting raises STR and DEX, casting and brewing raise INT. Each gain is announced in the journal.

Skills

Every skill trains by use and caps at 100. Gains come fastest when the task is a fair challenge for you.

Adventuring skills (your skill loadout)

Like gear, you equip the skills you want working. Equipped skills can add up to 700 points. Once your loadout is full, equipped skills stop improving until you unequip one. An unequipped skill keeps every point but does nothing until you equip it again, so you can swap between a warrior, mage or tamer build without losing progress. A skill at 0 can always be equipped. Trade skills (Lumberjacking, Mining, Fishing, Skinning) are always on and don't count toward the 700.

Training Grounds: on the Skills page, open the Training Grounds to drill any skill for free, up to 50. Tap Train (every tap counts: big gains while the skill is low, smaller near 50, about a minute or two of tapping per skill), or turn on AUTO and leave it running while you're in town (about 6 minutes per skill). It costs only time. Past 50, a skill grows only by using it for real: auto-gather and paid guild lessons stop teaching at 50 too.

Chivalry

Holy magic for the virtuous. It has its own blue tome in the Magic page's spellbook, costs mana only (no reagents), and trains the Chivalry skill. Its power follows your Karma: at Virtuous (+60) it is 40% stronger, and below -20 (Wicked) it fails outright.

The Thieves' Guild

Every Provisioner has a Back room tab: the Thieves' Guild. Join for 250 gold, then practise Lockpicking on three lockboxes (Simple, Sturdy, Masterwork), Snooping on a pouch-dummy with a bell, and Magery with the guild's hedge-wizard (40 gold a lesson). Practice teaches up to 70; past that, only real locks, pockets and spells will do.

The Town Hall quest board

Open it from MENU → Quests anywhere, or from the council table inside any Town Hall. It has three tabs: Town (wanted posters and town tasks), Weekly (the Hearthmoot's five weekly goals; finish them all for the Weekly Blessing) and Treasure maps.

  • Wanted posters: Murder Inc.'s three Blades are named killers, such as Varg "the Knife" Ostrel. Each poster shows their bounty and where they were last seen. Take one contract at a time; their trail shows at the top of your screen in the wilds. Kill them, then collect the bounty at the board. A Blade you bring in is hanged, and a new recruit with a new name takes their place. A Blade who kills you walks away, and their bounty grows.
  • Town tasks: slay a number of a monster or deliver goods (wood, ore, leather, fish). Progress shows at the top of the screen; collect your pay at the board. Finishing or abandoning a task brings new offers.

Treasure hunting

Tougher monsters sometimes carry a treasure map. Dungeon bosses often do, and the Tri-Wyrm always leaves a Legendary one. Maps come in five tiers: Tattered, Worn, Fine, Rare and Legendary.

  1. Decode it from MENU → Quests → Treasure maps. Cartography sets your chance; higher tiers are harder.
  2. Follow it: a gold X marks the dig site on your minimap and on the ground, and a line shows how many paces away it is.
  3. At the X, tap Dig for treasure. The chest breaks the surface and 1 to 3 guardians rise to defend it.
  4. Once they fall, Pick the lock (Lockpicking; keep trying if it holds), or Force it open: it always works, but a trap bites you and half the gold and a jewel are ruined.
  5. Loot it like a body: gold, reagents, bandages and jewelry with stat bonuses. Rare and Legendary chests add rare dyes. Every chest also scores War Week points on Hunt day.

Walk too far from a dug-up chest and it sinks again, but you keep the map and can dig again.

Raiders: Worn chests and better can be raided. Sometimes an orc war band from Grimtusk Hold or Murder Inc. cutthroats got there first: you find them looting it, or running off with the goods. They take most of the gold and a jewel. Cut them down: each one drops a pouch with its share, and the last one carries the jewel.

Bard skills

Three skills that work together: Musicianship (every song needs it; a bad roll and you fumble the tune), Peacemaking and Provocation. The songs are on your belt: a blue note for Peace and a red note for Provoke. After each song there's a 5-second pause.

You need an instrument. Buy one at the Provisioner: a Hand Drum (60 gold) lets you play at all, a Lute (280 gold) adds +6 to your Musicianship roll, and a Harp (900 gold) adds +12. Each one has its own sound when you play. The Peace and Provoke buttons only appear once you own one.

  • Peace: in a fight, it calms every monster on you and ends the fight. Out of a fight, monsters won't come for you for 6 to 20 seconds (longer with more Peacemaking). Rivals, Murder Inc. and dungeon bosses won't listen.
  • Provoke (wilderness fights): turns the monster you're fighting on another enemy, either one already on you or the nearest monster close by. For 8 to 18 seconds they fight each other, not you, and one may kill the other; you get the body. Stronger monsters are harder to provoke.

Hiding & Stealth

Out of a fight, tap the hooded eye on your belt to Hide. The better your Hiding, the more often it works, and it's much harder with an enemy close by. While hidden, monsters don't notice or bump into you, and rivals, Murder Inc. and ambushers pass you by.

Moving while hidden takes Stealth: every second on the move is a Stealth check, and failing one reveals you. With no Stealth at all, your first step gives you away. Gathering and casting reveal you too. Attack from hiding (tap a monster or press E) and your next landed swing is a surprise strike for 1.5x damage, rising to 2x with Stealth.

Trade skills

Lumberjacking, Mining, Fishing, Skinning, Blacksmithing, Carpentry, Tailoring and Alchemy are outside the budget and always count.

  • Tracking warns you when Murder Inc. is hunting you. At 30 it also marks hunters on the minimap.
  • Magic Resistance cuts the damage from the Tri-Wyrm's breath attacks, up to half.
  • Carpentry speeds up Settlement construction, up to 40% faster at 100.

Build suggestions

Your equipped skills can add up to 700 points, which is seven skills at 100. Here are some proven mixes to aim for. You don't have to commit: unequipped skills keep their points, so you can train several builds and swap between them on the Skills page. Get each skill to 50 at the Training Grounds first, then raise it the rest of the way by using it.

The Knight (sword, shield and holy light)

Swordsmanship, Tactics, Anatomy, Healing, Parrying, Chivalry, Magic Resistance

The sturdiest melee build. Tactics and Anatomy add to every hit, a shield plus Parrying blocks blows outright, and bandages with Healing and Anatomy keep you standing. Chivalry adds Consecrate Weapon for burst damage, Divine Shield and Close Wounds, with no reagents needed. Magic Resistance cuts spell damage and the Tri-Wyrm's breath. Keep your Karma up: Chivalry grows stronger at Virtuous and fails when you're Wicked. Put points into STR and DEX.

The Battle Mage

Magery, Eval Int, Meditation, Fencing (or Swordsmanship), Tactics, Anatomy, Magic Resistance

Opens with spells and finishes with steel. Eval Int multiplies your spell damage, and Meditation speeds up your mana regeneration. Carry reagents: each Magery cast in combat uses one. A good all-rounder once you have gold for reagents. Split points between STR and INT.

The Necro-Mage

Magery, Necromancy, Eval Int, Meditation, Magic Resistance, Wrestling, Hiding

A pure caster. Necromancy needs no reagents, so it carries you when your bags run dry: Grave Chill weakens a foe's blows, Raise Bonewalker raises a skeleton from a nearby body to hold the line, and Soul Siphon makes your hits heal you. Magery covers healing and heavy damage. Wrestling lets you fight back unarmed, and Hiding gets you out of a fight that goes wrong. Pile on INT.

The Tamer

Animal Taming, Animal Lore, Veterinary, Magery, Meditation, Magic Resistance, Tracking

Let your pets do the fighting. Taming needs both Taming and Animal Lore high enough for the creature, and pets gain levels as they fight beside you. Veterinary heals them, and at 80 it can bring a fallen pet back. Magery heals you and your pets from a safe distance. Tracking warns you when Murder Inc. is out hunting you. Bond with your best pet so it can't be lost for good, and use the stable to keep the rest.

The Ranger

Archery, Tactics, Anatomy, Healing, Tracking, Hiding, Stealth

A bow-and-bandage hunter who strikes from cover. Hide, creep in with Stealth, and your first hit from hiding is a surprise strike for extra damage. Tracking shows hunters on your minimap, and Healing with Anatomy keeps you going on long trips into the wilds. Favour DEX.

The Bard

Musicianship, Peacemaking, Provocation, Magery, Eval Int, Meditation, Magic Resistance

Controls the fight instead of taking hits. Peace ends a fight or keeps monsters off you, and Provoke turns a monster on its neighbour so they kill each other and you take the loot. Magery finishes off whatever is left. Buy the best instrument you can: the Harp adds the most to your songs. Rivals, Murder Inc. and bosses won't listen to your music, so keep an escape spell ready.

The Treasure Hunter

Cartography, Lockpicking, Fencing, Tactics, Anatomy, Healing, Poisoning

Built to decode maps, beat the guardians and open the chest cleanly. Cartography handles higher-tier maps, and Lockpicking opens chests without triggering the trap or losing half the gold. Poison your blade (a potion gives more hits the better your Poisoning) to wear down the guardians and any raiders who got there first. The Thieves' Guild teaches Lockpicking up to 70.

Starting out

  • New characters don't have 700 points yet, so equip what you use and fill the rest as you grow. Knight and Ranger are the easiest first builds; they need no reagents.
  • Trade skills (Lumberjacking, Mining, Fishing, Skinning, Blacksmithing, Carpentry, Tailoring, Alchemy) never count toward the 700, so any build can gather and craft.
  • Hunting the Tri-Wyrm? Swap Magic Resistance into your loadout first.

Combat

Walk up to a monster, or let it come to you. Once you're fighting, you swing automatically; your job is to pick targets, cast, heal and move.

Hitting and being hit

  • Your chance to hit compares your weapon skill with the monster's level. An even match is about 50%, and training the skill is what makes you land blows.
  • Damage comes from your weapon, plus bonuses from Tactics, Anatomy and Strength.
  • A monster's chance to hit you falls as your Dexterity rises. Armor reduces the damage.
  • Parrying with a shield equipped can block blows outright.

Packs

Hurt one monster and nearby members of the same kind may join in. Every orc belongs to one warband. Tap another monster, or press G, to switch targets.

Healing

  • Bandages: from the Provisioner (5 for 40g). Use them from the belt pouch or with B.
  • Heal potions: Lesser (15 HP), Heal (30), Greater (50). Brew them or buy them.
  • Mending Word and Greater Mending: heal spells you can cast in or out of a fight.

Loot

Defeated monsters leave a body. Tap it to open it: Loot All takes the gold, reagents, bandages and items, and Skin gives leather (furs from Ice Wolves). Magic jewelry is a rare drop, and a likely one from bosses.

Magic

There are two schools. Magery uses mana plus 1 reagent per cast in combat. Necromancy uses mana only. Put spells on your spell bar from the Magic page.

Out of reagents? The spell bar shows your reagent count and turns red below 5. Buy more at the Provisioner (5 for 5g), or switch on Auto-restock there to be topped up to 30 every time you enter a town.

Your chance to cast depends on Magery and the spell's circle. Circle 1 is dependable from the start, and each higher circle needs about 14 more Magery.

Gear, clothing & jewelry

Open Me from the MENU to see your paperdoll. Tap a slot, then tap a glowing item in your backpack to wear it. The paperdoll has three pages.

What you wear shows on your character in the world. Your weapon and shield are in your hands. Armor has real shape: leather and studded pieces fit close, while chain and plate add a breastplate with faulds, layered pauldrons, bracers, knee-cops and cuisses, each in its metal's color. Helmets, hats, cloaks, robes and dyed clothes show too; a robe goes over the armor.

  • Armor: head, neck, chest, arms, weapon, shield, hands and legs. Armor comes in Leather, Studded, Ring Mail, Chainmail and Plate.
  • Clothes: hat, shirt, robe, cloak, sash, pants and shoes. Buy or sew them at the Tailor.
  • Jewels: ring, bracelet and amulet.

Dyes

Dye clothing at the Tailor's dye tub for 15 gold a dip. There are 26 hues. The five rare ones (Blaze, Ice, Shadow, Gilded and Sea Foam) need 80 Tailoring, or a rare dye from commissions or the world boss.

Jewelry

A ring leans Strength, a bracelet Dexterity and an amulet Intelligence; bigger pieces sometimes spread into a second stat. The metal shows the tier: Copper, Silver, Gold, then Starmetal.

Gathering

Walk up to a glowing tree, rock or fishing spot in the wilds and tap the action button. Each gather takes about 3 seconds and trains Lumberjacking, Mining or Fishing.

  • Wood and ore feed crafting and Settlement construction.
  • Fish: stand at any shore (the sea, a lake or a river) and tap the rod on your belt, or fish the tidal pools on the Salt Coast. The sea gives mackerel, cod and the odd great tuna; rivers give trout and salmon; lakes give carp and pike. Big catches get more likely as your Fishing rises.
  • Ice is mined in the Frostwastes.
  • Leather and furs come from skinning bodies.

Once you have a Settlement, its Lumber Camp, Mine, Tannery, Herb Garden and Fishery bring these in for you, even while you're offline.

Pets & taming

Walk up to a wild creature and tap the action button to try to tame it. Success depends on your Animal Taming. A tamed pet follows you, fights beside you and grows stronger.

Follower slots: you have 5. Small pets (dog, wolf, bear) take 1 slot, mid-size ones (panther, bison, war horse) take 2, the sabertooth and griffin take 3, drakes and wyverns take 4, and a Forest Dragon takes all 5. On the Pets page, tap Follow or Stay to choose who comes with you: three wolves and a bear, or a single dragon. Your stable holds 15 pets; buy more slots on the Pets page with gold (up to 30).

Mounts: with a War Horse, Plains Bison, Dire Panther, Sabertooth Cat or Storm Griffin following you, tap Ride on your belt in the wilds. A horse runs at almost twice your speed and a griffin at twice; the big cats and the bison are a little slower. You leap down when a fight starts, and you leave your mount outside towns and dungeons. Not a tamer? The Pets page sells a riding horse for 400 gold.

Levels, bonding and death: pets take hits in a fight (tanks step in most). Every kill gives each following pet experience, and each level adds strength, health and fighting skill, up to level 30. After 20 fights at your side a pet bonds with you. An unbonded pet that falls is gone for good. A bonded one is only knocked out: revive it on the Pets page with 80 Veterinary, or pay the stable. Pets slowly mend between fights, and Heal uses your Veterinary.

Housing

Ten plots with for-sale signs are scattered across the wilds, and you can own one. Plots are 7×7 (2,500 gold) or 9×9 (5,000 gold), and one grand 12×12, the Kingswood Plot, costs 9,000.

  • Walk to your plot and tap the action button to design your house on a grid: floors, walls, windows and a door. You need a door to go inside.
  • Inside, place furniture and a storage chest. Any chest at home opens your bank box, the same one the Vaultkeep keeps, so your things are always in one place.
  • Bind Hearth on the House page, then Recall to Homestead from anywhere.
  • The crafting benches in your house open as your Settlement grows: the Forge opens the Blacksmith bench, the Lumber Camp the Carpenter's, the Tannery the Tailor's and the Herb Garden the Alchemist's.

The Settlement

Your plot grows into a walled settlement. Open it from House > Settlement. Buildings ring your house, settlers walk to work, and guards man the gate.

How it works

  • The Great Hall comes first. Its level (up to 10) unlocks buildings and caps their level.
  • One build at a time, on a real-time timer. Upgrades cost gold, wood and ore.

Settlers

Travelers you escort, and some you spare, may ask to settle on your land. From Great Hall 3, wanderers arrive on their own. Assign each settler to a building, or as a guard at the Barracks. Every settler is a natural at one building and works it at double pace.

Raids

Grimtusk orcs and Murder Inc.'s cutthroats raid about every one to two hours, and more often while you're at war with them. Raids don't start until you've built the Great Hall and you're no longer Young.

  • If you're nearby, raiders come through your gate at you, and your guards run in to fight beside you. Win to earn gold and Fame.
  • If you're away, your walls, guards and the Hall's townsfolk decide it. A win pays spoils. A loss costs 35% of stored goods and sets a building on fire until you repair it.
  • The Settlement page's Raids tab compares your defense with the raiders' expected strength, and lists recent news.

Guilds, warbands & wars

MENU → Guild has two things that are easy to mix up:

  • Your guild is online: other real players, the War Week standings, guild wars against other guilds, and the shared Guild Hall (Overview, Hall, Research, Help, Shop, Wars and Members tabs).
  • Your warband is your own hired companions (Warband tab). Once you own a house, raise one: hire warriors, archers and healers; take them along or leave them home to guard the Settlement. They level up from the fights they share.

Your warband can declare war on Grimtusk Hold or Murder Inc.. Kills against your enemy count toward victory, and winning a war pays 800 gold and renown. War cuts both ways: your enemy hunts you more often and raids your Settlement more.

The Guild Hall

Every online guild shares a Guild Hall (MENU → Guild → Hall, Research, Help and Shop tabs). The daily loop: spend your donations on research, help your guildmates' builds, open gifts, and spend merit in the shop. A red dot on a tab means something is waiting for you.

Research, and the Hall level it needs:

Threats & enemies

Threat colors

Every monster's nameplate and ground ring shows how the fight would go for you right now:

  • Trivial: ignores you
  • Easy
  • Fair fight
  • Tough
  • Deadly: run

Anything that's a real fight notices you from about 200 paces (less on the King's Road or while Young), shows a red “!”, and chases you. Trivial monsters leave you alone.

Enemies with a memory

  • Kael Vorn, Master of Murder Inc. lives in the world like you: gathers, hunts, sells, and grows stronger. He may come for you himself, and if he wins he takes some of your gear. Beat him to get it back. Each time he kills you he earns a darker title.
  • Murder Inc.: Kael Vorn's three named Blades hunt you once you're no longer Young; their wanted posters are on the Quest Board. Murder Inc. also hires highway cutthroats for raids. Train Tracking to hear them coming.
  • Orc war parties from Grimtusk Hold may burst out of the brush in the south.

Innocents

Travelers on the road can be helped, escorted, spared or robbed. Escorting and sparing raise Karma and can bring new settlers to your Settlement. Murdering innocents raises your Notoriety and makes you a target.

Dungeons

Walk into a dungeon's doorway to enter. Clearing its monsters builds up to its boss; take the stairs up to leave.

Most dungeon dwellers are sculpted and animated: the Whisper Crypt's dead, the Frostbound Tomb's frozen horrors, the Sunken Vault's Scalekin and their Sunken King, the Ember Depths' imps, golems and the Emberlord, and the Hollow's brutes and wraiths. They walk, charge, lunge and fall. Out in the wilds, so do the Rock Golems, Lesser Imps, Frostbitten Husks and the Sorrow Wraiths of the Fields of Sorrow.

Random bosses

  • The lair isn't always full. After you kill a dungeon boss, it usually returns in 4–6 minutes, but sometimes the lair lies quiet for 12–25 minutes.
  • Dread bosses. About one time in seven, a Dread version moves in (“The Dread Whisper King”). It's 10 levels tougher, and your journal warns you when one arrives. It carries double gold, a strong piece of magic jewelry, a treasure map and often a rare dye.
  • Breakouts. Every hour or two of play, the boss of a dungeon you've already unlocked breaks out and roams the wilds for 25 minutes. A banner and your journal announce it. It starts at its dungeon's door and shows red on the minimap. Kill it for a hoard: double gold, jewelry, a Rare treasure map, maybe a rare dye, a gift for every guildmate and Hunt points. If nobody gets it in time, it slinks back into its lair.

A three-headed dragon sleeps in the Cinder Caldera. Bring friends: your warband, pets and your raised bonewalkers all fight it with you.

Knowing when it wakes

  • 3 minutes before: the ground shakes, and a timer appears on screen (“Vyrathax wakes in 2:41”) with the direction and distance to the Caldera and an arrow pointing the way.
  • When it wakes: a banner and a message. The timer changes to “Vyrathax is awake!” and keeps pointing the way until you arrive.
  • On the maps: a pulsing orange arrow on the minimap's edge points to the Caldera, and the Caldera glows on the world map while the dragon is awake.
  • Directions: both maps have north at the top and show N, E, S and W. The small compass beside the minimap turns with your camera.

Each head holds a third of its health. The heads fall in order, and the survivors attack faster.

Its bite is weak; the breath attacks are what kill you, and Magic Resistance cuts them. If you retreat or die, its wounds stay, and it heals slowly (full again in about 17 minutes).

Rewards

  • Its skull hangs over your Great Hall

Death & the Young

When you fall you become a ghost. You can walk but touch nothing. After a short wait you're resurrected, or you can pray at a virtue shrine. Bless, potions and summoned allies end when you die.

New characters are Young for their first 20 minutes of play and for the whole First steps guide. While Young, Murder Inc., ambushes, orc war parties and Settlement raids leave you alone.

Tips & FAQ

My spells won't cast in a fight

You're probably out of reagents. Check the count above your spell bar, and turn on Auto-restock at the Provisioner.

I keep missing with my weapon

Your weapon skill is low for that monster. Fight Easy (green) monsters until the skill climbs, and train Tactics and Anatomy alongside it.

How do I make money?

Fill commissions, sell crafted gear, skin and sell furs in Frostmere, collect from your Settlement, and defend it from raids for spoils.

How do I get back to the game from a menu?

Tap the gold > Play button next to MENU.

Is my game saved?

The game saves itself every few seconds in your browser. If cloud saves are set up, MENU > Cloud save keeps your character across devices.
